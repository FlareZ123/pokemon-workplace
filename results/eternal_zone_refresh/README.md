# Eternal Zone self-suppression as a three-slot Bench refresh

## Question

Can an Eternatus VMAX deck deliberately turn off Eternal Zone, clean a full eight-slot Bench, and restore the Ability in the same turn?

## Mechanically valid line

Yes.

The bundled legal Expanded print `swsh3-117` Eternatus VMAX has Eternal Zone:

- if all of the player's Pokémon in play are Darkness type, the player can have up to eight Pokémon on the Bench;
- the player cannot put non-Darkness Pokémon into play;
- if the Ability stops working, the player discards Benched Pokémon until only five remain.

The legal Expanded Stadium Path to the Peak (`swsh6-148`) makes Pokémon with Rule Boxes in play have no Abilities. Eternatus VMAX has a VMAX Rule Box, so Path to the Peak suppresses Eternal Zone.

The legal Expanded Item Field Blower (`sm2-125`) can discard a Stadium in play, including the player's own.

This permits the same-turn sequence:

`full 8-slot Eternal Zone Bench -> play Path to the Peak -> Eternal Zone stops -> discard 3 Benched Pokémon -> Field Blower Path to the Peak -> Eternal Zone returns -> capacity 8`

If all five surviving Pokémon in play still satisfy Eternal Zone's Darkness-only condition, the restored Ability reopens three Bench slots.

## Core compatibility

This is the `C=8, c=5, L=8` case of the bulk-refresh model in `tools/bench_bulk_refresh.py`.

From a full eight-slot Bench, suppression forces three discards. If `R` Bench occupants are persistent core pieces and all other occupants are stale, the core survives intact whenever

`R <= 5`.

After Path to the Peak is removed, the five surviving occupants remain and Eternal Zone raises the capacity back to eight. The line therefore creates three fresh transactional slots.

Representative capacities:

| Persistent core occupants | Stale residents on full 8-slot Bench | Can all core survive? | Fresh slots after restoration |
| ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 3 | 5 | Yes | 3 |
| 4 | 4 | Yes | 3 |
| 5 | 3 | Yes | 3 |
| 6 | 2 | No, at least 1 core occupant must be discarded | 3 |

The final three open slots are mechanically identical in count. The strategic cost differs because a six-slot core cannot absorb the forced cleanup entirely with stale residents.

## Why this is stronger than the generic Stadium refresh

A normal Parallel City self-refresh moves a full five-slot Bench to three and later back to capacity five, reopening two slots while preserving at most a three-slot core.

The Eternal Zone line moves eight to five and back to eight, reopening three slots while preserving as many as five persistent core occupants.

The extra capacity comes from turning off and restoring the deck's own Ability rather than from removing a temporary smaller-cap Stadium.

## Action-resource profile

The line consumes:

- the Stadium play for the turn;
- one Stadium card, Path to the Peak;
- one Stadium-removal action, such as Field Blower;
- three chosen Bench occupants.

Field Blower is an Item, so Item lock removes this exact release edge. Lost Vacuum can provide another Item-based release line with a different cost. A Supporter or attack-based Stadium remover would have different timing and contention.

Path to the Peak also suppresses the player's other Rule Box Abilities while it remains in play. In this proposed refresh, that suppression is temporary because the Stadium is removed before further dependent actions.

## Strategic implications

### Bench cleanup can be generated by self-suppression

Ability lock is usually evaluated as something that removes options. Here, a temporary self-lock deliberately triggers Eternal Zone's built-in contraction clause and converts three stale Bench residents into three reusable slots.

### Ability suppression is path-dependent

After the three discarded Pokémon leave play, restoring Eternal Zone increases capacity but does not restore those Pokémon. The path

`8 -> 5 -> 8`

ends in a different board state from simply remaining at capacity eight.

### Connector evaluation needs side effects

A graph containing Path to the Peak and Field Blower might classify them only as Stadium disruption and Stadium removal. Their combined effect on Eternal Zone creates a board-reset function that neither card has in isolation.

This is an Archetype-Line-Specific interaction in the sense that it depends on Eternatus VMAX's exact capacity Ability and contraction text.

## Validation

`results/eternal_zone_refresh/reproduce.py` checks the three-slot refresh with the shared bulk-refresh kernel, verifies the five-core compatibility threshold, and confirms that a six-slot core necessarily loses one protected occupant.

## Limitations

This result establishes mechanical capacity and card-text compatibility. It does not establish that a competitive Eternatus VMAX list should include Path to the Peak or Field Blower for this purpose.

The line can also be disrupted by Item lock, Stadium-play restrictions, inability to access the required cards, Prize placement, or a surviving non-Darkness Pokémon that prevents Eternal Zone from reactivating. The three discarded occupants may contain attached resources whose loss dominates the value of the reopened slots.

A deck-specific simulation should evaluate how often three genuinely stale residents coexist with access to the two-card refresh package and a later use for the reopened slots.
