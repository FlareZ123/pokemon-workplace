# Counter Catcher's Prize-gated gust can disappear before its best turn

## Question

The earlier minimax work treated each gust as a freely executable Boss's Orders-like action once in hand. Counter Catcher is an Expanded-legal Item offering a similar targeted opponent switch, but its card text permits play only while **your remaining Prize cards exceed your opponent's**. Does taking Prizes during a gust sequence erase the tactical option to use a later copy?

## Rules and model

The bundled card pool contains Counter Catcher `sv4-160` and `sm4-91`; both require more Prize cards remaining than the opponent and switch a chosen opposing Benched Pokemon with the Active Pokemon. Boss's Orders `me1-114` or `swsh2-154` provides an unrestricted opposing Benched target through a Supporter. The advanced manual's B-01 and B-03 sections differentiate Item and Supporter play, and its A-01, D and E sections place KO and Prize-taking after attack declaration.

`tools/counter_catcher_prize_timing.py` adds the exact `own_prizes_remaining > opponent_prizes_remaining` condition to the original six-Prize, one-hit KO, adversarial-promotion minimax. The attacker starts with six Prizes, one opposing Active, 1..5 Benched Pokemon, and zero, one, or two Counter Catcher tokens representing pre-obtained, otherwise playable Items. After a KO, remaining own Prizes decrease by the target's 1/2/3 Prize value. Any later Catcher action checks the **updated** count.

Crucial controlled assumption: opponent's Prize count is held constant within the bounded target sequence. This isolates the self-disabling effect of the attacker's own Prize progress. In a live match, the opponent may take more Prizes, draw cards, evolve, bench additional Pokemon, or use other disruption. The model assumes every target is one-hit KO and that no further resource or lock prevents using the card.

## The timing paradox: preservation can destroy eligibility

Opponent Active **3 Prizes**, Bench **1 and 3 Prizes**, opponent has **3 Prizes remaining**. The attacker has six Prizes remaining.

- With one **Boss's Orders**, the attacker KOs the starting Active for three Prizes, then uses Boss next turn to gust and KO the other three-Prize target. It wins in **2 attacks**.
- With one or two **Counter Catchers**, using a Catcher immediately lets the defender choose the one-Prize Pokemon to promote, forcing a longer line. But preserving Catcher for the second turn also fails: after the natural three-Prize KO, the attacker has **3 Prizes remaining**, tied with the defender, so Counter Catcher is no longer playable. The minimax is **3 attacks**.
- If the defender instead has **2 Prizes remaining**, the same first KO leaves the attacker with three, still behind on Prizes, and Counter Catcher can be used on the next turn. Two attacks become possible.

The active target prize value is the trigger that changes action eligibility. This is a direct example of an opponent-independent tactical timing deadline generated by taking Prizes.

## A two-Catcher complementarity collapse

Opponent Active **1 Prize**, Bench **1, 3, 3 Prizes**. With two unconditional gusts the attacker can take the two three-Prize Bench targets on consecutive turns and win in **2 attacks**.

Two Counter Catchers yield that same two-attack route when the opponent has one or two Prizes left. With the opponent at three, four, or five, the first three-Prize KO closes the eligibility window, and the best adversarial-promotion line requires **4 attacks**. Both copies can be in hand and mechanically usable at the start, yet only the first relevant gust action remains executable in time.

## Validation

`results/counter_catcher_prize_timing/reproduce.py` implements an independent Boolean attack-deadline game tree. It checks **2,190** state/budget scenarios (146 boards times 5 opposing Prize-count scenarios times 3 Catcher counts) against the minimax recurrence. It asserts all histograms and attack sums, both witnesses, and monotonic domination by unconditional Boss gusts.

Run `python results/counter_catcher_prize_timing/reproduce.py` from repository root.

## Implications and next questions

Counting Counter Catcher as functionally equivalent to Boss's Orders in an endgame route can overstate access even when neither is Prized or locked. Its window is controlled by the **Prize trajectory** created by the player's own attacks.

A further model should allow the opponent's Prize count to change during opponent turns, attach those changes to survivability and expected attack choices, and include Supporter-vs-Item contention. Another high-value direction is a card-text classifier for other conditional gust resources such as Great Catcher, Serena, Toy Catcher, Custom Catcher, and Cross Switcher, preserving both target eligibility and payment restrictions.
