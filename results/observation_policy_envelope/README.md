# Observation-consistent policy envelopes and decision-sufficient signals

## Research question

The [binary information-gap result](../binary_choice_information_gap/README.md) gives an exact formula for two actions. Real Pokémon turn decisions often involve three or more legal actions, and simulators may incorrectly choose an action using hidden Prize information that has not yet been observed.

Can an exact finite model enforce what the player knows at decision time, and identify when a coarse signal yields the same choice value as a full deck inspection?

## Model

Each possible hidden state has an exact prior probability, a public **observation label**, and an ordered common menu of actions with explicit state-dependent payoffs.

An observation-consistent policy chooses one action per observation label. Its exact optimal expected payoff is the sum, over observation groups, of the highest probability-weighted expected payoff among actions within that group.

In symbols, where `g(s)` is the observation generated by hidden state s:

`V(g) = sum_o max_a sum_{s:g(s)=o} p(s) v(s,a)`.

The common action menu is important. If an action's effect can fail in a hidden state, that failure must be represented in the action's payoff for that state rather than retrospectively forbidding the action only in worlds where it is inconvenient. Hidden state should not silently act as observable legality information.

This model supports arbitrary numbers of actions, exact rational probability and payoff values, and different observation granularities.

## Pokémon example: one bit is enough to choose between Iono and N

Use the [Prize-uncertain Iono/N witness](../prize_informed_iono_n_choice/README.md): 46 old deck cards, six hidden Prizes, ten useful outs among deck and Prizes, a five-card old hand with one out, and a six-card redraw. The hidden count K of useful deck outs ranges from 4 to 10.

Optimal action by exact K:

- Choose **N** for K from 4 through 8.
- Choose **Iono** for K from 9 through 10.

That makes the binary observation `K >= 9` a **decision-sufficient signal** for this particular two-action endpoint. There is no additional action-choice payoff for knowing the exact K after the coarse signal is supplied.

| Knowledge at choice time | Expected immediate out access |
| --- | ---: |
| K0, no deck-out information | 74.232970% |
| Only whether K >= 9 | **74.401981%** |
| Complete K1 deck-out count | **74.401981%** |

The signal's prior positive probability is 67.551404%. That frequency is descriptive; its utility is determined by the action changes it enables.

A full deck search can reveal far more information than this one-bit statistic. Other goals, future-turn utility and physical search outputs can make the additional information valuable. Here it is **exactly sufficient for the specified local choice**.

## Multi-action witness

In a three-state toy belief with three equally likely states and three available actions, where action i pays one only in state i, a blind fixed choice obtains 1/3 expected payoff while complete revelation obtains 1. The binary absolute-difference theorem is inapplicable; the general envelope handles all three actions exactly.

## Implementation and validation

- `tools/observation_policy_envelope.py`: exact Fraction-valued belief/observation/action payoff compiler, enforcing a uniform action menu and unique state IDs.
- `results/observation_policy_envelope/reproduce.py`: independent brute-force enumeration of all possible observation-consistent action mappings for **2,560 three-state, three-action cases**, plus Iono/N K0, one-bit, full-K1 checks and the three-action toy.
- CI: `.github/workflows/validate-observation-policy-envelope.yml`.

## Limitations

The action menu is supplied by the caller. The implementation is an optimal decision evaluator once payoffs are known, and does not establish action legality, payability, lock interactions, actual deck search requirements or realistic full-game reward values.

An important next step is to attach physical connector transitions to observation groups, evaluating the changed hand/deck after paying rather than treating K1 as a free oracle.
