# Beheeyem recurring Mysterious Noise: a staged research synthesis

This page links a sequence of increasingly physical but still deliberately **conditional** studies of repeated Beheeyem (sm11-91) Mysterious Noise with Triple Acceleration Energy (sm10-190) in paper Expanded.

## Central lesson

A search path, card pool, or reusable attacking Pokémon does not establish that a deck can sustain consecutive turns of pressure. The full path consumes independent resources with different access deadlines:

**Establish Basics and an anchor -> acquire Stage 1 and TAE -> evolve, attach and attack -> recycle the first attacker -> reacquire a Stage 1 and TAE for another mature Elgyem -> survive opponent action denial and execute again.**

Each stage must be evaluated with physical Prize cards, card locations, real search payments, Trainer action classes and the timing of information. The program currently quantifies the middle acquisition stages; the outer game and real opponent remain unmodeled.

## Evidence progression

### 1. Basic setup and early cards

- [Beheeyem Poffin eligibility](../beheeyem_poffin_eligibility/) examines the exact first-turn Basic-access constraint under Buddy-Buddy Poffin, Battle VIP Pass and Nest Ball.
- [Third-Elgyem frontload](../beheeyem_three_elgyem_frontload/) establishes that early extra Basic staging can substitute for later tutor availability on certain continuation paths; deck objectives can reorder preferred search mixtures.
- [Turn-two packet access](../beheeyem_turn_two_attack_packet/) adds explicit early Beheeyem and TAE acquisition, reducing seemingly strong Basic-only results substantially.

### 2. Recycling a packet does not guarantee immediately retrieving it

- [Two-turn packet recycling](../beheeyem_two_turn_packet_recycle/) computes an exact conditioned first-packet rate of 12.006938% for B4/TAE4 in six residual T2 hand cards. One T3 natural draw gives 0.316352% **joint** first-and-second packet access. A singleton B plus singleton T is strictly unable to be reacquired together from one draw.
- [Multi-draw packet frontier](../beheeyem_multi_draw_packet/) generalizes to d T3 draws, using Prize-aware exact multivariate hypergeometric sampling. With B4/TAE4, joint access is 0.316352% at d1, 0.608267% at d2, and 1.896601% at d5. These d cards must still be *generated by real actions* in an eventual deck.

### 3. One physical tutor is far less than guaranteed search

- [Physical tutor frontier](../beheeyem_physical_tutor/) places one exact physical tutor into the 57 residual cards and permits search only when it is held or drawn, its target class matches, and sufficient neutral filler can be discarded. B4/TAE4/d1 joint access is 0.617735% with one Computer Search, versus 4.084632% if a free universal tutor is always guaranteed. The latter is an upper bound, not a realistic deck rate.
- Mysterious Treasure and Ultra Ball fetch Psychic Beheeyem; Guzma & Hala can obtain Special Energy under its optional discard payment. B1/TAE4/d1 strongly favors retrieving the limiting Stage 1 instead of another TAE in this specific physical access model.

### 4. Tutor use has a temporal opportunity cost

- [T2 rescue versus T3 defer](../beheeyem_tutor_timing/) allows a held tutor to rescue an otherwise incomplete T2 packet, correctly using a **47-card** post-attack T3 deck after searching rather than the **48-card** direct-first-attack deck. B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1: first attack access rises from 12.006938% to 16.893119%, but the joint two-attack event only rises from 0.617735% to 0.662553%. Early setup is an incomplete objective for recurrence.

### 5. Threatened Trainer lock can reverse the correct tutor timing

- [K1 prefetch under future lock](../beheeyem_prefetch_lock_policy/) compares saving a tutor to T2 prefetch when the first packet already exists. A hypothetical independent T3 Item-play prohibition with probability q can reward early Computer Search. B4/TAE4/d1 and certain prohibition: saved-tutor baseline 0.361169% versus K1-optimized T2 prefetch 0.562252%. At q=0, deferred universal search is at least as good throughout the tested finite grid.
- Category-limited tutor search has a small different phenomenon: early Ultra Ball can remove a spare Pokémon from the deck and improve the density of the unsearchable TAE before T3 draws, even without a lock. The measured B4/TAE4/d2 unlocked gain is only 0.002541 percentage points, so it should not be mistaken for a deck-building priority without other costs.



### 6. Knock Out awards can release packet cards previously trapped in Prizes

- [Prize-awarded packet continuation](../beheeyem_prize_packet/) integrates the complete six-Prize composition, uniformly chosen face-down Prize awards at the end of the first attack, and all T3 physically available packet/tutor/filler cards. Under the conditioned B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1 setup, awarding no Prizes gives **0.662553%** two-packet access; awarding one gives **1.125764%** and awarding two **1.679597%**. The reward count is an **exogenous conditional KO outcome**, not an attack KO rate. The experiment demonstrates that Prize progression can directly improve next-turn resource access.



### 7. Known Prize composition separates per-state Prize value from average draw value

- [K1 Prize pickup versus extra draw](../beheeyem_prize_vs_draw/) compares the same r=1 Prize pickup and an additional blind draw at fixed first-packet hand, full six-Prize composition and shuffled T3 deck. With B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1, 55.947740% of first-attack-capable K1 mass favors the Prize pickup, but its **aggregate** two-packet rate is 1.125764% versus **1.214069%** with an extra draw. Positive and negative state-specific gains have different magnitudes. A fully specified K1 state with three Prized B and three Prized T produces 100% packet access after one Prize and one draw, against 31/376 with two deck draws. Thus population-average draw quality can hide strategically decisive known-Prize states.

### 8. Opposing Iono reverses the short-horizon Prize-supply effect

- [Iono after Prize awards](../beheeyem_iono_prize/) directly models the opponent playing Iono on its reply: the player's current hand, including newly taken Prizes, is shuffled and bottomed; fresh top-deck draws equal **6-r** remaining Prizes, followed by the stipulated T3 draws. The earlier positive one-/two-Prize packet-access trend reverses for B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1: opposing-Iono two-packet access becomes **3.675774%** at r=0, **2.820953%** at r=1 and **2.019569%** at r=2. This is an exogenous, opponent-enabled Supporter response, not a statement that taking Prizes is bad or that Iono is a good strategic choice overall.



### 9. Hand-shuffle destination creates a Prize-dependent adversarial Supporter ranking

- [Opposing Iono, N and Marnie reset comparison](../beheeyem_supporter_reset/) distinguishes N's full-hand shuffle into the deck from Iono/Marnie's bottom-of-deck placement. N and Iono redraw according to remaining Prizes; Marnie redraws four for the opponent regardless of Prize count. For B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1, the three next-packet rates at r=0 are Iono 3.675774%, N 3.475483% and Marnie **2.019569%**; at r=2 they are Iono 2.019569%, N **1.881457%**, and Marnie 2.019569%. Across the complete finite 1,152-configuration grid, an opponent minimizing only this packet-access event would select **Marnie at r=0,1** and **N at r=2**. This is a narrow response rule, not global matchup strategy.

## What has been proved versus assumed

The listed numbers are mathematically exact **within their conditional probability models**, independently checked by physical-shuffle simulation and committed GitHub Actions where specified. The experiments do **not** currently provide actual match win rates or fully legal action-level play traces. The later models condition on an already staged, mature three-Basic board, all neutral filler discardable, no competing use of an ACE SPEC or Supporter, and exogenously provided T3 draws. The future-lock model additionally assumes K1 Prize knowledge and an exogenous lock probability independent of the player's policy.

The legal card-text and basic-rule context is in the repository resources. The program preserves the card pool's paper Expanded scope.

## Next milestones

A high-value extension is to remove the source-model shortcuts with a typed per-turn action executor, explicit evolving and attachment legality, attacker-to-anchor switch/replacement, other search actions, and actual opponent replies. The most important opponent question is whether it can establish Item or Supporter denial *after observing the T2 tutor decision*. The current fixed-q abstraction supplies a testable intermediate benchmark for a future adversarial model.

A second useful direction is realistic card discardability. Real hands contain singleton Pokémon, Tools, Energy and Supporters whose discard value depends on matchup and later turns. A two-discard tutor can therefore be unplayable despite being physically held, an effect absent from the generous filler-payment model.

The program's key methodological result remains: **physical access, action feasibility, and strategically valuable timing are separate variables**. A deck optimizer that merges them into one connector score can systematically overestimate renewable lock plans.
