# Beheeyem recurring Mysterious Noise: a staged research synthesis

This page links a sequence of increasingly physical but still deliberately **conditional** studies of repeated Beheeyem (sm11-91) Mysterious Noise with Triple Acceleration Energy (sm10-190) in paper Expanded.

## Central lesson

A search path, card pool, or reusable attacking Pokémon does not establish that a deck can sustain consecutive turns of pressure. The full path consumes independent resources with different access deadlines:

**Establish Basics and an anchor -> acquire Stage 1 and TAE -> evolve, attach and attack -> recycle the first attacker -> reacquire a Stage 1 and TAE for another mature Elgyem -> survive opponent action denial and execute again.**

Each stage must be evaluated with physical Prize cards, card locations, real search payments, Trainer action classes and the timing of information. The program currently quantifies the middle acquisition stages; the outer game and real opponent remain unmodeled.

## Evidence progression

### 1. Basic setup and early cards

- [Beheeyem Poffin eligibility](../beheeyem_poffin_eligibility/) examines the exact first-turn Basic-access constraint under Buddy-Buddy Poffin, Battle VIP Pass and Nest Ball.
- [Third-Elgyem frontload](../beheeyem_three_elgyem_frontload/) establishes that early extra Basic staging can substitute for later tutor availability on certain continuation paths; deck objectives can reorder preferred search mixtures.
- [Turn-two packet access](../beheeyem_turn_two_attack_packet/) adds explicit early Beheeyem and TAE acquisition, reducing seemingly strong Basic-only results substantially.

### 2. Recycling a packet does not guarantee immediately retrieving it

- [Two-turn packet recycling](../beheeyem_two_turn_packet_recycle/) computes an exact conditioned first-packet rate of 12.006938% for B4/TAE4 in six residual T2 hand cards. One T3 natural draw gives 0.316352% **joint** first-and-second packet access. A singleton B plus singleton T is strictly unable to be reacquired together from one draw.
- [Multi-draw packet frontier](../beheeyem_multi_draw_packet/) generalizes to d T3 draws, using Prize-aware exact multivariate hypergeometric sampling. With B4/TAE4, joint access is 0.316352% at d1, 0.608267% at d2, and 1.896601% at d5. These d cards must still be *generated by real actions* in an eventual deck.

### 3. One physical tutor is far less than guaranteed search

- [Physical tutor frontier](../beheeyem_physical_tutor/) places one exact physical tutor into the 57 residual cards and permits search only when it is held or drawn, its target class matches, and sufficient neutral filler can be discarded. B4/TAE4/d1 joint access is 0.617735% with one Computer Search, versus 4.084632% if a free universal tutor is always guaranteed. The latter is an upper bound, not a realistic deck rate.
- Mysterious Treasure and Ultra Ball fetch Psychic Beheeyem; Guzma & Hala can obtain Special Energy under its optional discard payment. B1/TAE4/d1 strongly favors retrieving the limiting Stage 1 instead of another TAE in this specific physical access model.

### 4. Tutor use has a temporal opportunity cost

- [T2 rescue versus T3 defer](../beheeyem_tutor_timing/) allows a held tutor to rescue an otherwise incomplete T2 packet, correctly using a **47-card** post-attack T3 deck after searching rather than the **48-card** direct-first-attack deck. B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1: first attack access rises from 12.006938% to 16.893119%, but the joint two-attack event only rises from 0.617735% to 0.662553%. Early setup is an incomplete objective for recurrence.

### 5. Threatened Trainer lock can reverse the correct tutor timing

- [K1 prefetch under future lock](../beheeyem_prefetch_lock_policy/) compares saving a tutor to T2 prefetch when the first packet already exists. A hypothetical independent T3 Item-play prohibition with probability q can reward early Computer Search. B4/TAE4/d1 and certain prohibition: saved-tutor baseline 0.361169% versus K1-optimized T2 prefetch 0.562252%. At q=0, deferred universal search is at least as good throughout the tested finite grid.
- Category-limited tutor search has a small different phenomenon: early Ultra Ball can remove a spare Pokémon from the deck and improve the density of the unsearchable TAE before T3 draws, even without a lock. The measured B4/TAE4/d2 unlocked gain is only 0.002541 percentage points, so it should not be mistaken for a deck-building priority without other costs.



### 6. Knock Out awards can release packet cards previously trapped in Prizes

- [Prize-awarded packet continuation](../beheeyem_prize_packet/) integrates the complete six-Prize composition, uniformly chosen face-down Prize awards at the end of the first attack, and all T3 physically available packet/tutor/filler cards. Under the conditioned B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1 setup, awarding no Prizes gives **0.662553%** two-packet access; awarding one gives **1.125764%** and awarding two **1.679597%**. The reward count is an **exogenous conditional KO outcome**, not an attack KO rate. The experiment demonstrates that Prize progression can directly improve next-turn resource access.



### 7. Known Prize composition separates per-state Prize value from average draw value

- [K1 Prize pickup versus extra draw](../beheeyem_prize_vs_draw/) compares the same r=1 Prize pickup and an additional blind draw at fixed first-packet hand, full six-Prize composition and shuffled T3 deck. With B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1, 55.947740% of first-attack-capable K1 mass favors the Prize pickup, but its **aggregate** two-packet rate is 1.125764% versus **1.214069%** with an extra draw. Positive and negative state-specific gains have different magnitudes. A fully specified K1 state with three Prized B and three Prized T produces 100% packet access after one Prize and one draw, against 31/376 with two deck draws. Thus population-average draw quality can hide strategically decisive known-Prize states.

### 8. Opposing Iono reverses the short-horizon Prize-supply effect

- [Iono after Prize awards](../beheeyem_iono_prize/) directly models the opponent playing Iono on its reply: the player's current hand, including newly taken Prizes, is shuffled and bottomed; fresh top-deck draws equal **6-r** remaining Prizes, followed by the stipulated T3 draws. The earlier positive one-/two-Prize packet-access trend reverses for B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1: opposing-Iono two-packet access becomes **3.675774%** at r=0, **2.820953%** at r=1 and **2.019569%** at r=2. This is an exogenous, opponent-enabled Supporter response, not a statement that taking Prizes is bad or that Iono is a good strategic choice overall.



### 9. Hand-shuffle destination creates a Prize-dependent adversarial Supporter ranking

- [Opposing Iono, N and Marnie reset comparison](../beheeyem_supporter_reset/) distinguishes N's full-hand shuffle into the deck from Iono/Marnie's bottom-of-deck placement. N and Iono redraw according to remaining Prizes; Marnie redraws four for the opponent regardless of Prize count. For B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1, the three next-packet rates at r=0 are Iono 3.675774%, N 3.475483% and Marnie **2.019569%**; at r=2 they are Iono 2.019569%, N **1.881457%**, and Marnie 2.019569%. Across the complete finite 1,152-configuration grid, **among these three candidate Supporters**, an opponent minimizing only this packet-access event would select **Marnie at r=0,1** and **N at r=2**. See section 12 for the stronger Judge/Ace Trainer candidates. This is a narrow response rule, not global matchup strategy.



### 10. Opponent Supporters have hidden-state reversals even with identical post-N decks

- [Known-state N versus Iono reversal](../beheeyem_reset_state_value/) partitions the same finite hand/Prize states by their relative chance of yielding the next packet after an opposing N or Iono. B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1/r1 has **N better for Beheeyem in 26.640302% of first-attack-capable K1 probability mass**, despite N having lower average packet access (2.647861% versus Iono 2.820953%). Two precise witnesses have the same 52-card post-N shuffled population B4/TAE4/C1/F43, giving identical 19.111035% N packet access. Their Iono probabilities differ: **4.655832%** if the old hand held 3 TAE + Computer Search, versus **22.535055%** if it held four filler. An opponent generally cannot observe this hidden-state distinction; a usable policy needs a belief state.

### 11. Revealing Prize positions can make critical earned Prizes selectable

- [Mapped face-up Prize selection](../beheeyem_mapped_prizes/) evaluates the information effect of Town Map (bw7-136), which makes Prize cards face-up. Under a free-information upper bound, choose an optimal r-card Prize subset before T3 random draws rather than selecting uniform face-down positions. B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1: r=1 raises joint two-packet access from **1.125764% blind** to **2.926368% mapped**; r=2 raises it from **1.679597%** to **4.452270%**. Actual Town Map acquisition, card slot and Item-play costs are excluded, so these numbers are information ceilings. A subsequent opponent Iono/Marnie bottoming the newly chosen cards erases their immediate top-draw benefit within the current model.

### 12. Expanded counterplay action set: Judge and Ace Trainer

- [Judge and Ace Trainer opposing hand-reset frontier](../beheeyem_judge_ace_response/) expands the prior N/Iono/Marnie comparison to two more legal Supporters. Judge shuffles both hands **into** the deck and gives the Beheeyem player four cards. Ace Trainer is playable by the opponent only when it has **more Prizes remaining**, then gives Beheeyem three cards. For B4/TAE4/Computer Search/d1 at r=0, **Judge 1.899188%** narrowly outperforms Marnie 2.019569% as a disruption source; Ace Trainer is ineligible. At r=1, **Ace Trainer 1.198389%** is stronger than Judge 1.890141%, while N yields 2.647861%. For all 1,152 exact parameter configurations, including direct-first and paid-tutor-first branches separately, Judge is at least as disruptive as N/Marnie, and Ace Trainer is at least as disruptive as Judge when eligible. This **revises the three-candidate best-response statement** in section 9. It still ignores the opponent's actual Supporter access and own Prize count beyond an assumed six remaining.

### 13. Physical opponent Supporter access limits the counterplay

- [Basic-conditioned opposing Supporter reachability](../beheeyem_opponent_supporter_access/) couples an independent illustrative 60-card opponent deck (12 Basics, four Judge, four Ace Trainer, 40 fillers) with the conditional Beheeyem packet engine. An accepted seven-card Basic-containing opener, two more deck draws, six Prizes and zero to two subsequently earned opposing Prizes determine each physical Supporter's availability. Ace Trainer is legal only while its player has strictly **more Prizes remaining** than Beheeyem. For B4/TAE4/C1/d1 when Beheeyem took two Prizes and the opponent still has six, hypothetical always-available Ace Trainer yields **1.192659%** joint packet access; with actual opponent access and rational choice between held Judge/Ace or no reset, this increases to **1.450037%**. Using a public signal that the first attack consumed the singleton tutor reduces it to **1.436326%**. At r1/q6, a hidden-route aggregate policy leaves the hand alone at **1.125764%**, whereas a route-aware policy selectively plays Ace on direct-first-packet situations to reach **1.089143%**. The full exact 1,152-grid and independent physical-shuffle simulation [passed CI 38133018466](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38133018466). All are conditioned source-access figures, with opponent deck construction and actual board actions highly abstracted.

### 14. Opponent self-setup creates a two-objective response frontier

- [Opposing hand-reset self-target tradeoff](../beheeyem_opponent_self_target/) adds a hypothetical four-copy opponent target X and exactly models whether Judge/Ace's redraw yields it. The 60-card opponent deck becomes Basic12/Ace4/Judge4/X4/filler36, with the same Basic-conditioned opener, two draws, own Prize awards, and public first-tutor-use signal. The opponent maximizes `P(own X after response) - lambda × P(Beheeyem next packet | first attack)`. At Beheeyem r1/opponent q6, pure X optimization (lambda0) gives 59.871555% own-X readiness but permits a 1.252222% Beheeyem joint packet event. Strong packet-denial emphasis reduces both to 44.337605% own-X and 1.089143% Beheeyem joint event. These are explicit scalar sensitivity calculations, **not calibrated win rates**; exact K0 formulas and 480k independent physical shuffles [passed CI 38133471853](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38133471853).
- [Exact rational policy thresholds](../beheeyem_opponent_frontier/) calculates all pairwise expected-utility line crossings and removes non-optimal transitions. With r1/q6, the public-route-aware policy has **eight distinct weight intervals**, starting at the exact threshold `800168127187301020206/207970721609353909375 ≈3.847504`; an aggregate-route policy has only three intervals and starts changing near 5.448856. Opponent observed first-packet tutor use changes the action policy's frontier structure. [CI 38133656250](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38133656250) passed ten exact frontiers.

### 15. Opponent K1 knowledge of its own Prized target can improve reset selection

- [K1 target-Prize information value](../beheeyem_opponent_k1_target/) compares the same opponent's K0 policy (unknown exact remaining X Prize count) with free K1 knowledge after a hypothetical prior deck search. A K1 hand reset draws X with exact probability `1 - C(N-(4-z),n)/C(N,n)` where `z` copies remain Prized and `N=53+s` after discarding the Supporter; the K0 probability is its Prize-posterior average. With r1/q6/lambda10 and public tutor route, X readiness changes from 56.410922% (K0) to 56.310040% (K1) while Beheeyem's joint packet event falls from 1.144932% to 1.142429%; the weighted utility increases by 0.000472574. The information source is **free in this study** and the gain is small, but this supplies an exact belief-state/line-selection benchmark. Posterior identities, 60 policy checks and physical Prize-shuffle Monte Carlo [passed CI 38133827205](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38133827205).

### 16. Exact maximum and achievable paid K1 information under Item-lock deadline

- [Global exact K1 information-value ceiling](../beheeyem_opponent_k1_information_frontier/) enumerates all rational response-action utility crossings and proves the maximum free Prize-X information advantage over every nonnegative denial weight. With Beheeyem r1/opponent q6 and no tutor-route observation, the maximum utility gain is 0.006253694 at lambda≈81.677800; with visible tutor route the maximum is 0.002776303 at lambda≈98.731704. At a fixed lambda10 the information roles interact differently: route-hidden K1 gain is only 0.000000681, route-aware K1 gain is 0.000472574. This is a scalar policy-bound result, not a win rate, [CI 38134055377](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38134055377).
- [Payable Ultra Ball search before Item lock](../beheeyem_prelock_k1_search/) gives the opponent a separate illustrative 60-card deck B12/U4/F32/Ace4/Judge4/X4. Conditioning on a Basic-valid opening and a physically held Ultra Ball with **two disposable filler payments**, a full-deck search creating K1 is feasible in **35.393362%** of seven-card openers and **46.477077%** after two extra prelock draws. Four further cards drawn only after Beheeyem's Item lock would have raised the counterfactual paid Item search probability to 62.252256%, but those new sources cannot be played while Item-locked. Exact staged two-zone enumeration and physical shuffles [passed CI 38134274680](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38134274680).
- [Two-stage paid Ultra Ball decision](../beheeyem_paid_k1_policy/) propagates the **real U+F+F discard**, full-deck inspection, post-search thinning (53→50 later shuffled cards), K1 target-Prize inference, next reply draw, public first Beheeyem tutor route, and later Judge/Ace/no-reset policy. At lambda10, Beheeyem r1/opponent q6, zero further opportunity cost tau, only **26.220950%** of all legal prelock hands actually use Ultra Ball, despite 46.477077% physical eligibility; expected utility rises by 0.002432814. With tau0.01 use falls to 12.216193% and gain to 0.000994933, and with tau0.1 use falls to zero. These are exogenous-r-informed upper-bound early choices, verified [CI 38134590567](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38134590567).
- [Reward-prior early source policy](../beheeyem_paid_k1_reward_prior/) removes the previous shortcut that the earlier Ultra Ball choice knows Beheeyem's future first-attack Prize reward. Under a uniform rational prior over r=0/1/2 at lambda10 and tau0.01, an actually prior-aware early policy yields utility improvement **0.000194717**, while clairvoyant future-r knowledge adds another **0.000407206**. Crucially, at tau0 the uniform-prior overall +0.001760650 utility improvement decomposes as **+0.001693196 due to physically removing U+F+F from the eventual redraw population** and only **+0.000067454 due to learned Prize-X composition**. Most of the gain comes from deck-material concentration, exposing an information-attribution trap. Exact degenerate-prior equivalence checks and 4 mixed prior families [passed CI 38134795752](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38134795752).

### 17. Exact paid-source opportunity-cost thresholds

- [Exact rational early source-cost frontier](../beheeyem_paid_k1_cost_frontier/) extends the future-Prize-prior decision model by deriving the entire optimal Ultra Ball purchase policy across every nonnegative additional source cost tau, instead of choosing several arbitrary test weights. Every prelock hand class s has an exact rational benefit `b_s` from physically paying Ultra Ball, reducing the deck by three cards, and learning K1 own-X Prize composition. At cost tau the source is chosen exactly when `b_s>tau`; consequently `G(tau)=sum_{b_s>tau}w_s(b_s-tau)` and source incidence `R(tau)=sum_{b_s>tau}w_s`, with piecewise-affine gain slope `-R`. At lambda10 and a uniform future r0/r1/r2 prior, there are just **three positive rational source-cost breakpoints**, the highest near **tau=0.011593924**, beyond which buying the early source is never optimal. Its physical eligibility is 46.477077% of Basic-valid nine-card early hands, but the optimal source use is 26.220950% at tau0 and 12.216193% at tau0.01. Four priors, 41 exact policy cross-checks, [CI 38135559832 passed](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38135559832). Utility units and r prior are hypothetical, with no actual win-rate mapping.

### 18. Separate draw geometry of discard payment and two-piece requirements

- [Target-hit probability under typed discards](../discard_target_hit_geometry/) proves that nondecreasing remaining target density is sufficient to guarantee the chance of seeing one target in n without-replacement draws does not fall. But density falling need not reduce hit: N7/K2/n3, discard three including one target gives density 2/7→1/4 yet hit probability 5/7→3/4. Exact 21,980-case finite census found 528 density-decrease/hit-increase counterexamples, [CI 38135051295 passed](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38135051295).
- [Two-distinct-piece packet hits](../discard_joint_packet_geometry/) require keeping the B and T category counts separately. At N53/B4/T4 and six draws, P(both) is 13.655279% normally; deleting three filler raises it to 15.121096%, whereas discarding one B and two filler lowers it to 11.848201%, and one B+one T+one filler to 9.279679%. Even a higher combined B-or-T density can correspond to zero joint success after a required singleton category is deleted. Exhaustive 199,536-state check and 600k independent physical shuffles [passed CI 38135211721](https://github.com/FlareZ123/pokemon-workplace/actions/runs/38135211721). These exact formulas are reusable parts of ALS/AMR-aware resource engines, not whole attack/KO models.

## What has been proved versus assumed

The listed numbers are mathematically exact **within their conditional probability models**, independently checked by physical-shuffle simulation and committed GitHub Actions where specified. The experiments do **not** currently provide actual match win rates or fully legal action-level play traces. The later models condition on an already staged, mature three-Basic board, all neutral filler discardable, no competing use of an ACE SPEC or Supporter, and exogenously provided T3 draws. The future-lock model additionally assumes K1 Prize knowledge and an exogenous lock probability independent of the player's policy.

The legal card-text and basic-rule context is in the repository resources. The program preserves the card pool's paper Expanded scope.

## Next milestones

A high-value extension is to remove the source-model shortcuts with a typed per-turn action executor, explicit evolving and attachment legality, attacker-to-anchor switch/replacement, other search actions, and actual opponent replies. The most important opponent question is whether it can establish Item or Supporter denial *after observing the T2 tutor decision*. The current fixed-q abstraction supplies a testable intermediate benchmark for a future adversarial model.

A second useful direction is realistic card discardability. Real hands contain singleton Pokémon, Tools, Energy and Supporters whose discard value depends on matchup and later turns. A two-discard tutor can therefore be unplayable despite being physically held, an effect absent from the generous filler-payment model.

The program's key methodological result remains: **physical access, action feasibility, and strategically valuable timing are separate variables**. A deck optimizer that merges them into one connector score can systematically overestimate renewable lock plans.
