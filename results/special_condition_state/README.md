# Special Condition payloads and Pokémon Checkup scheduling

## Question

Is a Pokémon's Special Condition state fully represented by a set of condition names, and can Pokémon Checkup be modeled as an arbitrary interleaving of status processing and triggered effects?

Implementation: `tools/special_condition_state.py`  
Regression: `results/special_condition_state/reproduce.py`

## Evidence from the bundled Advanced Player's Rulebook

The v3.4 manual gives several state distinctions that matter mechanically:

- an Asleep or Paralyzed Active Pokémon cannot announce an attack and cannot retreat normally;
- moving an Active Pokémon to the Bench clears all Special Conditions;
- evolving an Active Pokémon clears its Special Conditions while preserving attached cards and damage;
- Pokémon Checkup processes Special Conditions in the fixed order `Poisoned -> Burned -> Asleep -> Paralyzed`;
- when several Trainer-card or Ability effects apply during Pokémon Checkup, the player whose turn would be next chooses their order and, for each effect, whether it resolves before or after **all** Special Conditions;
- irregular Special Conditions can change the base number of damage counters, and a later regular or irregular version of the same condition replaces the previous one;
- when several active effects replace a Special Condition's base damage-counter amount, only the highest replacement applies once;
- effects that add more counters are additive, and they apply after the base replacement.

The manual's examples expose the ordinary numeric bases needed by this narrow model: Poisoned uses 1 damage counter, Burned uses 2, and Confused uses 3 when its attack-attempt penalty applies.

## Finding 1: a set of condition names is state-aliasing

`tools/board_object_kernel.py` currently stores:

```text
special_conditions: frozenset[str]
```

That is sufficient for checks such as "is the Active Asleep?" and for clearing all conditions on switching or evolution. It is insufficient for irregular condition payloads.

Two states can both project to:

```text
{"Poisoned"}
```

while one is ordinary Poisoned and places 1 counter during Checkup, and the other is Galarian Weezing's `Severe Poison` version and places 4 counters.

The reproducer confirms that both states have the same legacy name projection while producing different counter amounts. Therefore the name set is not a sufficient mechanical state representation once irregular Special Conditions are in scope.

## Additional basic-rules authority

The bundled advanced manual intentionally focuses on deeper mechanics and does
not restate every basic Special Condition coexistence rule. The official
Pokémon TCG rulebook fills that gap:

- Asleep, Confused, and Paralyzed share the rotated-card state; whichever
  happened last is the only one of those three that remains;
- Poisoned and Burned use markers and coexist with the rotated-card condition;
- Poisoned and Burned can also coexist with one another.

Official rulebook source:
https://assets.pokemon.com/assets/cms2-en-uk/pdf/trading-card-game/rulebook/par_rulebook_en.pdf

This authority required a correction to the first version of the regression,
which had allowed one Pokémon to be both Confused and Paralyzed.

## Finding 2: the condition instance needs a local payload

The minimal representation added here stores one `ConditionInstance` per condition kind:

- condition identity;
- condition-local base damage-counter amount when that condition has one;
- optional provenance label for debugging/evidence.

Applying a newer instance of the same condition kind replaces the old instance.
This directly represents the manual's regular/irregular replacement rule.

The basic-rules authority adds one cross-kind invariant: applying Asleep,
Paralyzed, or Confused clears either of the other two if present. Poisoned and
Burned remain independently stackable. The state validator rejects manually
constructed states that violate the rotated-card exclusivity rule.

## Finding 3: condition payload and external modifiers are different layers

A condition-local payload is not the same state as a persistent modifier generated by another card.

For Burned, the reproducer composes:

- ordinary Burned base: 2;
- Pyroar `Scorching Aura`: replace the base with 4;
- Infernape `Flaming Fighter`: replace the base with 6;
- Magmortar `Magma Surge`: add 3.

The manual explicitly validates `Scorching Aura + Magma Surge = 7` counters.

With all three modifiers active, the same rules derive 9 counters:

`max(2, 4, 6) + 3 = 9`.

This derived case illustrates why a simulator should keep:

1. condition-local payload;
2. active base-replacement modifiers;
3. active additive modifiers;

as separate inputs to the transition.

## Finding 4: the Checkup condition sequence is one scheduling block

The manual does not give the ordering player permission to insert a Trainer/Ability Checkup effect between Poisoned and Burned, or between any other two Special Conditions.

Instead, each such effect is placed either before or after checking **all** Special Conditions, while the effects themselves can be ordered by the player whose turn would be next.

For `n` distinct Checkup effects, the unconstrained abstract scheduling space is therefore equivalent to permuting `n` effects plus one atomic Special Condition block:

`(n + 1)!` schedules.

Examples:

- 0 effects -> 1 schedule;
- 1 effect -> 2 schedules;
- 2 effects -> 6 schedules;
- 3 effects -> 24 schedules.

The reproducer enumerates these schedules and expands the atomic block only after placement. It asserts that the internal condition order remains contiguous and fixed.

This is a useful pruning rule for a game engine: an arbitrary interleaving model would explore illegal orderings and overstate decision freedom.

## Finding 5: Confused belongs to a different timing family

The manual's attack procedure checks Confused during the attack attempt, after other attack-attempt conditions and before the attack's later resolution steps. Pokémon Checkup's condition list contains Poisoned, Burned, Asleep, and Paralyzed, but not Confused.

The kernel therefore keeps Confused as a valid `ConditionKind` and numeric payload state while excluding it from `checkup_conditions()`.

This avoids the common modeling error of treating every Special Condition as a Checkup transition.

## Architectural consequence

A larger simulator can preserve the current board-object movement API while replacing the lossy name set with a typed status layer.

A safe authority split is:

- board object: which Pokémon currently carries which condition instances;
- Special Condition state: condition-local payload and same-kind replacement;
- continuous-effect layer: active base replacements and additive modifiers;
- Checkup scheduler: authority and before/after placement around the atomic condition block;
- condition resolver: basic-rule coin flips and recovery behavior;
- KO subsystem: damage-counter consequences and Knock Out timing.

The last two layers are intentionally outside this result because the bundled advanced manual does not fully restate every basic-rule Special Condition procedure.

## Validation

The regression checks:

- ordinary Poisoned and Severe Poison alias under the legacy name projection but differ mechanically;
- latest regular/irregular same-kind application replaces the previous payload;
- the rulebook's 4-plus-3 Burned example equals 7 counters;
- multiple base replacements collapse to the highest amount while additive modifiers stack;
- applying Paralyzed replaces Confused in the rotated-card condition slot;
- applying Asleep then replaces Paralyzed while preserving Poisoned and Burned;
- Confused is excluded from the Checkup condition sequence;
- the condition block preserves the rulebook order among conditions that are legally present;
- two Checkup effects produce exactly 6 abstract schedules;
- three Checkup effects produce exactly 24 abstract schedules;
- no enumerated Checkup effect appears inside the Special Condition block.

## Limits

This result does not yet implement the full basic-rule recovery mechanics for
Burned, Asleep, Paralyzed, or Confused. Cross-kind coexistence is now enforced
from the official basic rulebook, while recovery coin flips and turn-sensitive
Paralysis recovery remain separate work.

It does not resolve Knock Outs, effects triggered by damage-counter placement, Ability suppression, or continuous-effect eligibility changes during Checkup. Those are composition problems for the existing board, trigger, lock, and KO kernels.

The `(n + 1)!` schedule count is the pre-pruning choice space for distinct eligible effects. Card dependencies can make some schedules semantically equivalent or cause later effects to cease being eligible.

## Next useful integration

A natural follow-up is a typed adapter between `BoardPokemon` and `SpecialConditionState` so existing movement/evolution transitions clear the richer condition objects without losing payload identity. After that, the Checkup scheduler can be composed with `effect_order_authority.py` and the trigger-deferral kernel to model condition-driven triggers without interrupting an effect already resolving.
